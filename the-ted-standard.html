The TED Standard

A lot of good words end in TED.

TrusTED. EducaTED. DedicaTED. CommunicaTED. AnticipaTED. AppreciaTED. It started as a joke about my name. It became the six-part checklist I run every single loan against — because if you can't say all six at the closing table, I left something on the field.

Why this exists

The mortgage industry optimized for speed. It forgot about the person.

Apply in eight minutes. Upload your documents to a portal. Get an automated status email every Tuesday. On paper it's efficient — and for a simple file it works fine.

Then something happens. The appraisal comes in short. An underwriter asks a question nobody warned you about. Your closing date moves and nobody calls. Suddenly you're the one chasing your own loan.

The TED Standard is what I put in place of that. It's not a marketing slogan — it's six specific commitments, each one something you can hold me to and notice when it's missing.

And each one, conveniently, ends in TED.

"Who do I even call about this?"
"Why didn't anyone tell me sooner?"
"Is this a good deal or just a fast one?"
"Am I just a file number here?"
The six

From "I have no idea what I'm doing" to "we've got this."

01

TrusTED

"I believe what he's telling me."

Everything else here is worthless without this one. You'll get straight answers, including the unprofitable ones — "that payment is going to stretch you," "wait six months and fix this first," "the other offer you got is actually better." I'd rather lose a loan than earn one by staying quiet.

02

EducaTED

"I understand my options and what they cost."

There's rarely one right way to structure a mortgage. Down payment size, loan type, points, term, timing — they all trade off against each other. I lay them out in plain English with real numbers attached, so you're choosing between options instead of being handed one. If a term doesn't make sense to you, that's my job to fix, not yours to Google.

03

DedicaTED

"Someone worked harder than they had to."

This is the part clients mostly don't see. Running your file three different ways to find the structure that actually saves money. Calling the listing agent personally so your offer carries weight. Pushing a condition through on a Friday afternoon instead of letting it sit until Monday. Bare minimum is a choice — and it's not one I make.

04

CommunicaTED

"I never had to wonder where things stood."

You'll hear from me at every milestone, and you'll hear specifics: what happened, what it means, what's next, and when. Not "your loan is in process." If you ever feel the need to chase me for a status update, I've already failed this one.

05

AnticipaTED

"The problem got handled before it became a crisis."

Most mortgage emergencies were visible weeks earlier to someone who was looking. A deposit that will need explaining. A closing date that doesn't line up with your lease. A gift letter nobody asked for yet. I look ahead on purpose, so the surprises get solved while they're still small.

06

AppreciaTED

"I was treated like a person, not a transaction."

Nobody has to choose me. Every client and every referral is a decision somebody made, often on a friend's recommendation with their own reputation attached. I don't take that lightly — during the loan or years after it, when you call with a question and I still pick up.

The communication promise

You shouldn't have to ask me what's happening.

Every lender says they communicate well. This is what the difference looks like in your inbox.

The update most people get

"Your loan is still in process. We'll let you know."

The update I send

"Appraisal came back at value — no issues there. Underwriting has the file and asked for two things: your June bank statement and a quick letter about the deposit on the 12th. Send those today and we stay on track for Friday's closing. I'll confirm the minute they're cleared."

Specific. Proactive. Sent before you had to ask. That's the whole standard in one message — and it's what fanatical communication actually means in practice.

Before you apply

A real conversation about what you're trying to do, what it will cost, and whether now is even the right time. No credit pull required to have it.

While you're shopping

A pre-approval that holds up, and a lender your agent can call directly when an offer needs backing at 9pm on a Sunday.

In underwriting

Milestone updates with specifics, conditions explained in plain language, and problems flagged by me first — with the plan already attached.

After closing

An annual look at your rate and equity, and a number that still works when life changes. The relationship doesn't end at the signing table.

What to expect

Clear expectations. On both sides of the deal.

If you're my client

  • A real conversation before any recommendation
  • Every option explained in plain English, with the tradeoffs
  • Honest advice — including "not yet" when that's the truth
  • Proactive updates, before you have to ask for them
  • One person who owns your file from hello to keys
  • A relationship that continues long after closing

If you're a real estate agent

  • Pre-approvals a listing agent can actually trust
  • Status updates sent to you, not just your client
  • A phone call to the listing side to strengthen the offer
  • Problems flagged early and solved, never hidden
  • On-time closings treated as the standard, not the goal
  • Clients who come back to you happy about the process
When something goes wrong

Real estate deals hit bumps. Here's what happens when mine do.

Appraisals come in short. Underwriters ask for one more document on the day you're packing boxes. Sellers get difficult. Anyone promising you a perfectly smooth transaction every single time is selling something.

What I promise instead: you'll hear it from me first, and it will come with a plan attached. Bad news doesn't get buried here until it becomes an emergency — it gets owned, explained and solved.

That's usually the moment clients understand why any of this matters. Anybody looks good when everything goes smoothly.
